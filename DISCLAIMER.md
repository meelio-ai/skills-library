They are not intended for use by patients or the general public, and they must not be used as a substitute for consultation with a qualified healthcare provider.

## 2. Decision support — not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment

These skills produce **supportive analysis for clinician review**. They do not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease, and their outputs are not medical advice. Every output must be independently reviewed, verified against source data, and accepted, modified, or rejected by the treating clinician, who retains full and sole responsibility for all clinical decisions. AI systems make mistakes: they can misread values, miss context, and state incorrect information with confidence. Never act on an AI-generated interpretation without verifying it against the original report and the patient in front of you.

## 3. Functional and optimal ranges

Many skills in this library reference "functional" or "optimal" ranges used in integrative and functional medicine practice. These ranges differ from — and are typically narrower than — the standard laboratory reference intervals validated by the performing laboratory. They are not universally accepted by conventional medicine, are not a standard of care, and vary between practitioners and populations. Skills present them alongside standard ranges as a lens for clinical consideration, not as a replacement for the laboratory's validated interval. Apply the standards appropriate to your license, jurisdiction, and patient.
